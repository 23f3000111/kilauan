The story of keringkam

Gold thread on sheer gauze, stitched by the Malay women of Sarawak for well over a century. This is its story, and the story of the woman who stitches every Kilauan piece.

1841 to 1946The Brooke era

An adornment of Kuching's upper class

Keringkam is a traditional heritage embroidery of the Malay community of Sarawak, with its history traced to the Brooke era. Intricately handcrafted by generations of Malay women, it is distinguished by delicate patterns worked in shimmering gold or silver metallic thread.

Once regarded as an adornment of the upper social class, it was worn as a mark of elegance and prestige. In her 1913 memoir of life in Sarawak, Ranee Margaret Brooke recalled the women of Kuching wearing the tudung keringkam.

The craftTwo techniques

Counted, pierced and buried

The gauze is hemmed and stretched on a wooden frame, the pedangan. Motifs are placed by counting the threads of the fabric, so every star, petal and border sits true.

The thread itself is a flat strip of gold- or silver-coated metal. It pierces the gauze and is buried with each stitch, a technique embroiderers call tikam timbus. Borders are worked first, then the field. A single piece can take from one to six months.

The formsSelayah and selendang

Worn for the occasions that matter

The keringkam veil comes in two forms. The selayah is the smaller veil, worn over the head and resting on the shoulders. The selendang is wider and longer, draped from the head to the waist. Both are paired with the traditional Malay baju kurung.

Keringkam is worn for weddings, engagements, the completion of the Al-Quran and other prestigious celebrations. Today its artistry has also found its way into table runners, framed artworks, cushion covers and bookmarks.

The artisanNorinda K. Su'ut

Master artisan, cultural bearer, heritage advocate

Every Kilauan piece is created by Norinda K. Su'ut and carries hours of patience, skill, devotion and love. No two pieces are ever truly identical. Each is handcrafted to keep the character of the tradition while making something uniquely meaningful for its owner.

2024 to 2026Passing it on

From one hand to the next

Norinda teaches keringkam to anyone patient enough to learn it. Her classes start with the materials and the frame, and by the third week students are working the tali air border. University students in her MPU 3442 class have finished samplers of their own and prepared a special project with the community at Lubok Antu.

Through National Training Week she has taught free courses at UNIMAS in Kuching and Samarahan, and in 2025 she joined a study visit to Kayseri University in Türkiye, sharing keringkam with students of traditional crafts.

Bersatu hati dan bekerjasama. "Work together, with one heart, if we want to protect the keringkam we love."
