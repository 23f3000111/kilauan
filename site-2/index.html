In her words

Jaga dan pelihara warisan Sarawak untuk anak cucu kita. "Look after Sarawak's heritage, and keep it for our children and grandchildren." Norinda, after a class in 2026.

Norinda makes keringkam, the gold-thread embroidery of Sarawak's Malay community, by hand and to order. A single selayah can take months: in 2022 she finished her fourth of the year stitching only at weekends, slowly but surely.

Classes and lessons

Learn it
stitch by stitch

This is how Norinda's classes unfold, from a student's first threaded needle to a finished sampler.

See classes

01Week one

The basics

Getting to know the materials and the frame before the first stitch.

  • The materials of keringkam
  • Sewing the edges of the selayah or selendang
  • Threading the flat strip through the needle
  • Mounting the gauze on the pedangan

02Week two

The right technique

By the second week, students stitch with the correct technique. Neatness comes with practice, and Norinda's advice is simple: embroider with the right technique.

  • Counting the threads of the gauze
  • Even, neat stitches in gold

03Week three

The tali air border

In the third week, students try "membalas tali aek", working the flowing water-channel border along the gauze.

  • Working a continuous border
  • Turning neat corners

04Finished

A sampler of their own

Students in Norinda's MPU 3442 class finished samplers of their own in June 2025, then prepared a special project with the community at Lubok Antu.

From Kuching to Kayseri

In 2025 Norinda joined a study visit to Kayseri University in Türkiye, at its Mustafa Çıkrıkçıoğlu Vocational School. She met teachers and students of traditional handicrafts and tried their braiding and needlework for herself.

It is the furthest keringkam has travelled with her so far, and a reminder that heritage grows when it is shared.

Care information

Keep it for generations

Keringkam is a delicate, handcrafted heritage piece. With the right care, it can be treasured and passed down.

Do not wash

No washing or dry-cleaning. Cleaning can damage the delicate fabric and the metallic embroidery.

Roll, never fold

Roll it gently around clean, acid-free paper. Store it flat or rolled, in a drawer or box of its own.

Keep away from

Direct sunlight, moisture, perfume and fragrance, chemicals, detergents and cosmetics.

Handle with care

Always use clean, dry hands. Avoid pulling, pressing or placing heavy items on the embroidery.

Need personal advice on handling or preserving a piece? Ask the assistant, or message Norinda.
